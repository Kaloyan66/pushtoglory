The Cinematic AI Film Workflow

Full worlds, locked characters, real stories. How a brand film gets made by one operator, in the right order, with no set, no crew and no location permits.

11 min read Claude · Higgsfield · Seedance 2.0 · CapCut Intermediate

      There used to be a gap between what you could imagine and what you could afford to shoot. Most brand ideas died quietly inside that gap. This is the workflow that closes it: a full cinematic world, with characters, action and atmosphere, produced by one operator.

      One pretty shot was never the hard part. Holding a character, a look and a world steady across an entire story was. For years every AI film died the same way: scene one stuns, scene two drifts, scene three belongs to a different movie.

      The fix is not a better model. It is the order you work in. Story first. World second. Cast third. Shot list fourth. Renders last.

      01 · Why it breaksFilms are held, not generated

      Watch any AI short that actually works. What impresses you is not any single frame. It is that the thing holds.

      Here is how most attempts fall apart:

      1. There is an idea, and rendering starts immediately.
      2. No fixed world rules, so every prompt reinvents the look.
      3. No cast sheets, so every scene imagines the character again.
      4. No shot list, so the story only exists in the operator's head.
      5. Every render is beautiful on its own and incoherent together.

      Rendering is the cheapest and least important part of this workflow. The craft did not disappear. It moved to the decisions you make before the first render: what the story is, what the world looks like, and what the camera points at.

      02 · The rulesThe 5 holds

      A film is a system of holds. Audit every scene against all five.

      #HoldWhat it meansWhere it breaks
      1CharacterSame face, wardrobe and energy in every sceneNo cast sheet, characters described again from memory
      2LookSame palette, grain and grade across shotsNo World Bible, so every shot grades itself
      3WorldSame physics, geography and logicLocations that remodel between scenes
      4LightLight sources that stay consistent and motivated"Cinematic lighting" used as a prompt
      5StoryEvery shot advances the same arcRendering before the shot list exists

      The math

      A cinematic brand film the traditional way means director, DP, cast, permits and a post house. Tens of thousands, weeks of lead time, and one shot at getting it right.

      This workflow: one operator, an afternoon of world building, then shots that render in minutes. If the film does not work, you change the script and render again. A bad creative decision drops from a reshoot to a retype, and your time goes to story and taste, the two things no tool supplies.

      04 · The workflowThe workflow, step by step

      Six steps. The first four produce no video at all, and that is deliberate. By the time you render, every decision that could break the film has already been made in text, where changing your mind is free.

      Phase one · Decide the film in words
      01
      Story

      The story spine

      A brand film is an ad with an arc. Lock the spine in a Claude chat before any visuals exist:

      Paste into a new Claude chat
      Write a story spine for a [15/30/60] second cinematic
brand film for [BRAND / PRODUCT].

Structure:
1. HOOK (first 3 seconds): an image or action that raises
   a question
2. TENSION: the want, the obstacle, the motion
3. TURN: the moment the product enters or resolves it
4. PAYOFF (last 5 seconds): the emotional landing and the
   end card

Rules: one protagonist, one want, one location change at
most, no dialogue unless one line can carry the film.
Write it as 4 numbered beats, one sentence each.

      If the spine does not work as four plain sentences, no amount of rendering will save it. Iterate here. It costs nothing.

      If your protagonist wants something concrete, also run the five sentence spine gate from the Film System: want, obstacle, decision, consequence, change. The four beats give you the ad's shape. The gate proves there is a story inside it.

      You now haveFour sentences that already read like a film.
      02
      World

      The World Bible

      The film's look, fixed in words. Every shot renders against this.

      Paste into the same chat
      Create the World Bible for this film.

PALETTE: [3 to 4 colours that own the frame]
LIGHT: [hard or soft, the sources, time of day, mood]
LENS: [e.g. 35mm handheld intimacy, or 85mm compression
with slow push-ins only]
TEXTURE: [film grain or clean digital, atmosphere: rain,
dust, neon haze]
WORLD RULES: [what exists in this world, and what never
appears in it]

Restate this World Bible at the top of every shot prompt
you write for me, word for word.

      The World Rules line matters most. A world defined only by what is in it looks generically epic. A world that also says what never appears in it looks like somebody's film.

      You now haveA World Bible Claude will paste into every prompt, word for word.
      03
      Cast

      The cast sheets

      Same discipline as the rest of the Vault: a character reference on a plain background, front and 3/4 views, a full physical description, the exact wardrobe, generated in Higgsfield and re-rolled until it is exact. The full sheet prompts are in The Locked-Cast Production System, steps 02 to 04. Build the sheets there, use them here.

      Cast deliberately. The protagonist is your customer, not a generic attractive face. If the viewer cannot see themselves in the lead, the film is about somebody else.

      You now haveA saved cast sheet for every character, attached as @image1, @image2 and so on.
      04
      Plan

      The shot list

      Films drift when shots are improvised one at a time. Build the whole list before rendering anything:

      Paste into the same chat
      Build the shot list for this film as a table:

| # | Beat | Action (one beat) | Camera | Motion | Sound |

Rules: 2 to 5 seconds of screen time per shot. One action
per shot. Every shot serves the spine. Mark which shots
show the product. For a 30 second film expect 8 to 12
shots. In the Sound column, only sound with a visible
cause in frame. Score is added in the edit.

      Read the table like an editor. Can you see the film cut together? Fix it in the table, not in the renders.

      You now haveA numbered shot list that plays in your head before anything renders.
      Phase two · Render and cut
      05
      Render

      Per-shot prompts

      Every prompt carries the anchors:

      The per-shot template
      16:9. [4 to 6] seconds. One continuous shot.

REFERENCES: @image1 = [character], identity lock.
@image2 = [product or second character].

WORLD BIBLE: [paste word for word]
CHARACTER (@image1): [paste the cast sheet description
word for word]

ACTION: [one beat from the shot list]
CAMERA: [framing, lens and movement from the shot list]
SOUND: [ambience and any sound with a visible cause]

Hold the character's identity exactly as @image1. Follow
the World Bible palette and light. Natural motion physics:
real weight, ground contact, natural cloth movement. No
music, no text on screen.

      Two things are deliberately missing. There is no shot number, because the model cannot see the other shots and numbering only adds noise. And there is no score direction, because music inside a video prompt either does nothing or bleeds into the picture. Score goes on in the edit.

      Render in shot list order. Seedance 2.0 renders at least 4 seconds, so render each beat a little long and trim to the moment in the edit. Audit each shot against the cast sheet and the World Bible before moving to the next one. Fix drift by tightening the anchors, not by re-rolling and hoping.

      06
      Cut

      The edit pass

      AI footage becomes a film in the cut.

      1. Cut on action, not on shot ends. Trim the first and last tenth of most renders.
      2. Grade every shot to the World Bible palette in one pass. This single step unifies renders that came out slightly different.
      3. Sound design carries realism. Room tone, footsteps, cloth movement under the score. Silence reads as AI.
      4. Titles and the end card in brand type. The end card is the only place allowed to look like an ad.
      You now haveA finished film with one grade, a sound bed, a score and an end card.

      05 · TemplatesFour World Bible starters

      Paste one into step 02 and adjust the details to your brand.

      Luxury minimal (fragrance, jewellery, skincare)
      PALETTE: bone white, warm sand, deep espresso, one gold accent
LIGHT: soft directional daylight, long shadows, late afternoon
LENS: 85mm, shallow depth, slow push-ins only
TEXTURE: clean digital with faint grain, still air
WORLD RULES: no clutter, no text in the world, matte surfaces,
every frame could be a print ad
      Street documentary (streetwear, energy drinks, apps)
      PALETTE: concrete grey, sodium orange, black, one brand colour
LIGHT: available light only, night streetlights, hard contrast
LENS: 24mm handheld, close and inside the action
TEXTURE: visible grain, motion blur allowed, light rain
WORLD RULES: real city logic, crowds stay anonymous, nothing
looks staged
      Warm domestic (food, home, family products)
      PALETTE: cream, honey wood, sage green, terracotta
LIGHT: morning window light, soft and motivated
LENS: 35mm at eye level, gentle drift, no dramatic angles
TEXTURE: soft digital, steam and dust in the light
WORLD RULES: lived in but tidy, real kitchens not showrooms,
hands do real work
      High fashion editorial (apparel, beauty campaigns)
      PALETTE: monochrome plus one violent accent colour
LIGHT: single hard source, sculptural shadows
LENS: 50mm, symmetry allowed, bold negative space
TEXTURE: crisp, high contrast, zero atmosphere
WORLD RULES: minimal sets, poses held a beat too long, fabric
always in motion

      06 · CraftMaking the product the protagonist

      The difference between a tech demo and a brand film is the answer to one question: whose story is this?

      • The product enters at the turn, not in every frame. One product moment beats five product shots.
      • The protagonist is your customer, cast on purpose on the sheet.
      • The world is your brand's world: the palette of your site, your packaging, your feed.
      • If you removed the product, the film should collapse. If it does not, the product was wallpaper.

      08 · When it breaksTroubleshooting

      ProblemFix
      The film feels like disconnected clipsThe shot list is missing or ignored. Rebuild it and re-render only the shots that break the arc.
      The character changes between scenesThe cast sheet is not attached or the description is too vague. Fix the sheet and restate the anchors word for word.
      The look shifts shot to shotThe World Bible is missing from the prompts. Paste it into every single one.
      Everything looks generically epicYour World Rules are empty. Say what never appears in this world.
      Motion feels floatyAdd physics: real weight, ground contact, natural cloth movement.
      Music bled into the pictureScore direction leaked into a prompt. Keep sound diegetic and add the score in the edit.
      The film is pretty but boringThe spine failed. Go back to four sentences and find the tension.

      09 · AvoidWhat not to do

      • Don't render before the spine, the World Bible, the sheets and the shot list exist. Rendering first is how films die.
      • Don't write "cinematic lighting" and hope. Name the source, the direction and the time of day.
      • Don't change worlds mid film. One location change at most in 30 seconds.
      • Don't let dialogue carry what an image can. One spoken line at most unless the concept needs more.
      • Don't skip sound design. Footage without room tone reads as AI instantly.
      • Don't grade shots one by one. One palette pass across the whole film.
      • Don't stretch to 60 seconds because you can. The 15 second version is usually the better film and the better ad.

      10 · RhythmThe monthly cadence

      WeekActionOutput
      Week 1Spine, World Bible and cast sheets for the month's filmThe world exists
      Week 2Shot list, every render, continuity auditThe footage exists
      Week 3Edit, grade, sound, end card. Ship the hero film.The film is live
      Week 4Cut three or four short versions (6, 10, 15 seconds) for placementsThe film becomes a campaign

      One world, built once, produces a hero film and a month of cutdowns.

      11 · StartYour first film

      Write the four sentences. Fix the world in words. Lock the cast on plain backgrounds. Build the shot list like an editor. Only then render, in order, auditing every shot. Cut on action, grade to the palette, and add the sound the footage forgot.

      The barrier to telling a story on film has dropped to almost zero. What is left is the part that was always rare: having something to say, and the taste to say it cleanly.
