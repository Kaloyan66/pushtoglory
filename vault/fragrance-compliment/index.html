The Fragrance Compliment System

The full breakdown of the Madawi White ad, and the system for producing twenty "someone asked what I'm wearing" moments a week.

      A woman tells the camera that three people this week asked what she is wearing. Then she names the fragrance. The whole ad is a normal person casually vouching for a bottle, and it is the kind of clip that makes someone stop scrolling and go buy.

      You cannot smell an ad. Every fragrance brand fights that, and most fight it with abstraction: deserts, silk, slow motion. Beautiful, and it proves nothing.

      The compliment hook works because it replaces smell with the thing people actually buy fragrance for: being noticed. "Three people asked what I'm wearing" is not a scent claim. It is witnessed social proof. The viewer does not need to smell anything to believe the only thing that matters. People notice this.

      01 · Why it worksSell being noticed, not the smell

      A real creator ad like this costs hundreds per video and weeks per round. Find the creator, ship the bottle, brief them, wait, hope. One clip, one moment, one bet.

      This is not one video. It is a system for producing social proof at volume. Twenty people, twenty different "someone asked" moments, twenty angles tested in a week.

      Three things carry the whole format:

      • A specific witness. "Three people this week" is a scene. "People keep asking" is invisible.
      • The name, said once and clean. It is the moment people replay, so it has to land.
      • Scent in a person's words. Nobody talks about sillage in a voice note.

      02 · The skeletonThe four beats of the actual ad

      The skeleton fits one Seedance 2.0 generation at around 14 seconds, or two clips with a B-roll between them if you want a longer version.

      The Madawi White skeleton
      BEAT 1 · THE COMPLIMENT
"Three people this week asked what I'm wearing."
A specific number plus a specific timeframe is believable.
Vague is invisible.

BEAT 2 · THE NAME DROP
"Madawi White by Arabian Oud."
Clean, once, no repetition. People replay this moment, so
the lip sync has to hold here.

BEAT 3 · THE NOTES, SPOKEN LIKE A PERSON
"Pear, coconut, soft musk."
Three notes at most. Zero perfume counter language.

BEAT 4 · THE WEAR CLAIM AND THE VERDICT
"It just stays on you. Easily my favourite this year."
One practical claim, one personal verdict. Done.

      The math

      The creator route: hundreds per clip, weeks per cycle, one moment per spend.

      This system: an afternoon for a batch and every witness moment you can imagine. Most brands still pay for one clip and pray. Make twenty and let the data pick the winner.

      Keep it honest

      Base every claim on the real product: its real notes and what real wearers say about how long it lasts. Follow your ad platform's rules on labelling AI generated people, and do not present an AI person as a real customer. The compliment format works because it is believable, so keep it believable.

      05 · The workflowThe workflow, step by step

      Four steps. Script, cast, render, test. Five witness moments in one afternoon.

      Phase one · Script and cast
      01
      Script

      The master script prompt

      Paste into Claude
      Write a spoken fragrance clip of 30 to 35 words using the
compliment skeleton:

1. Compliment moment: [NUMBER] people in [TIMEFRAME] asked
   about it, told casually with a small smile
2. Name drop: [FRAGRANCE + HOUSE], said once, clean
3. Notes: [3 NOTES], spoken like a person describing a
   taste, not a counter assistant
4. Wear claim and verdict: [PRACTICAL CLAIM] plus a personal
   favourite line

Rules:
- Contractions, casual energy, one small laugh or pause
- Banned: "fragrance journey", "sillage", "olfactory",
  "notes of", "evokes", "sophisticated"
- Say "this" and "it", not the category name
- She is telling a friend, not selling
- The wear claim must be true for this fragrance

Then write the same clip with the hook swapped for each of
these witness moments: [PASTE THE FIVE BELOW].
      You now haveFive scripts on one skeleton, each with a different witness.

      Cast

      The casting prompt

      Generate one clean reference of the creator first: plain grey background, waist up, minimal makeup, natural skin. That is @image1. The bottle on a real surface, label readable, is @image2.

      Paste into Seedance 2.0 in Higgsfield

continuous take, no cuts, no text on screen.

Woman (@image1), [AGE RANGE], [SPECIFIC face: skin tone,
visible natural skin texture, eye colour], [HAIR], minimal
makeup, wearing [CASUAL SPECIFIC GARMENT] and small
everyday jewellery.

Bottle (@image2): [FRAGRANCE], [bottle shape, colour, cap,
the label spelled out], held loosely in one hand.

Setting: by a window, soft daylight from the side, a normal
room behind her, slightly blurred. Framed like a self
recorded phone clip: eye level, close, a little casual.

She speaks to camera with easy, unbothered energy and a
small smile at the compliment. Spoken words:
"[SCRIPT]"

Realism anchors: natural skin texture with no smoothing, a
real shadow from the window, slightly off centre framing,
natural blinks, lip sync matched to every word and exact on
the name, a tiny pause before the verdict.

      Casual is the brief. The clip should feel recorded between errands, not produced. If it looks expensive, it stops working.

      You now haveOne reference per persona and a filled prompt for each script.
      Phase two · Render and test
      03
      Render

      Render and check the name drop

      Render each script as one clip. Then check the one thing people replay: the name. If the lip sync slips on the fragrance name, regenerate with "lip sync exact on the name" and slow that line down slightly in the script.

      When a persona has more than one clip, keep her voice identical: extract the audio from her first approved take and attach it as @audio1 on every clip after it.

      04
      Test

      The batch and test protocol

      Week one: render the five witness moments below with one persona. Deploy at equal budget to the same audience. Review at 48 hours against starting thresholds, calibrated to your account over time: kill under a 25% hook rate or above 1.4x your target cost per purchase, scale above a 35% hook rate or below target cost.

      Week two: take the winning moment and rotate the persona instead. Different ages, styles and energy, the same moment. Now you know both the moment and the messenger your market responds to.

      Week three on: twenty clips a week is one afternoon. Rotate moments, personas and verdict lines. The feed never sees the same clip twice, and every clip is the same proven skeleton underneath.

      You now haveThe winning moment, the winning messenger, and a weekly rotation built on both.

      06 · TemplatesFive witness moments

      Same skeleton, same product, a different witness. Rotate these:

      The week counter
      Hook: "Three people this week asked what I'm wearing. Three."
Energy: amused disbelief, counting on her fingers.
      The elevator
      Hook: "My coworker followed me into the elevator just to ask
what I was wearing."
Energy: slightly embarrassed, pleased.
      The gym
      Hook: "A stranger at the gym took out an earbud mid set to
ask what I was wearing."
Energy: an incredulous laugh.
      The next day text
      Hook: "My date texted me the next morning. Not about the
date. About the perfume."
Energy: a raised eyebrow, mock offence.
      The barista
      Hook: "The barista asked. The barista."
Energy: deadpan, then a smile.

      07 · When it breaksTroubleshooting

      ProblemFix
      The hook feels flatThe number or the timeframe is missing. Make the moment a scene, not a summary.
      She sounds like a commercialBanned words leaked in, or the energy is too polished. Rescript with a pause and a small laugh.
      The name drop is unclearRegenerate with "lip sync exact on the name" and slow that line slightly.
      The notes beat dragsYou have more than three notes. Cut to three.
      The clip looks too prettyReduce the set: a normal room, one window, no golden glow.
      Winners fatigue after two weeksRotate the moment and keep the persona. Then rotate the persona and keep the moment.

      08 · AvoidWhat not to do

      • Don't stack claims. One wear claim only. More sounds like an ad.
      • Don't say the category. "This" and "it" work better than naming the product type.
      • Don't use counter language. "Sillage" has never been said in a voice note.
      • Don't over produce. Window light and a normal room, or it reads as a commercial.
      • Don't drop the number and the timeframe from the hook. "People keep asking" is invisible. "Three people this week" is a scene.
      • Don't let the name drop mumble. It is the moment people replay.
      • Don't run one clip. The system is the batch.

      10 · StartYour first five

      Write the compliment. Drop the name once. Three notes, said like a person. One wear claim, one verdict. Cast her by a window and keep it casual. Then do it again with different witnesses and let the data crown the winner.

      You cannot smell an ad. But you can watch someone get noticed, and that was always what the bottle was for.
