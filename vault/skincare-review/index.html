The Hyper-Real Skincare Review System

How the skincare review made with no creator was built, beat by beat, and how to produce a whole cast of skin stories on demand.

      A woman with no makeup talks to her phone about why her skin always looked tired. How she kept piling on highlighter to fake a glow and it just looked cakey. Then she found the cream. No camera, no creator, no product shipped to anyone.

      What sells a skincare review is not the claim. It is the texture around the claim. The freckles. The uneven window light. The way she trails off at the end of a sentence. Nobody notices these consciously, but everyone's brain files the clip as "real person" or "ad" within two seconds, and everything after that verdict is either testimony or noise.

      Most brands still buy this one creator at a time: a few hundred per video, two weeks of shipping and briefing, one skin type, one story, one bet. This page replaces the bet with a test. Four faces, four skin stories, and the market tells you which one it believes.

      The math

      The creator route: a few hundred dollars per clip, two weeks of shipping and briefing, one skin type per spend, and the clip you get is the clip you get.

      This system: an afternoon and a full cast of skin stories. A failed test costs a retype. You stop hoping one creator's face matches your market and start letting the market vote between four.

      Keep it honest

      Social proof only works if it is built on something true. Write every script from what real customers actually say (the angle mining step in the UGC Factory does exactly this), make only claims the product can back, and follow your ad platform's rules on labelling AI generated people. In many markets, presenting an AI person as a real customer who used the product counts as a fake testimonial. The format multiplies whatever you put into it, including trouble.

      03 · The workflowThe workflow, step by step

      Five steps. Script, cast, render, check, test. Four finished reviews in an afternoon.

      Phase one · Script and cast
      01
      Script

      The four beats

      This is the structure behind the ad, beat by beat. The four beats split cleanly into two parts, and each part fits one Seedance 2.0 generation (up to 15 seconds).

      The beats
      PART 1 (about 12 seconds)

BEAT 1 · THE TIRED PROBLEM
"My skin always looked tired. Like, always."
The felt problem in the customer's words. Not "dull
complexion". Tired.

BEAT 2 · THE FAILED FIX
"I kept piling on highlighter to fake a glow and it just
looked... cakey."
One specific thing she tried that did not work. This beat
does the heavy lifting: it proves she is one of us, not a
brand.

PART 2 (about 12 seconds)

BEAT 3 · THE DISCOVERY
The product enters casually. What actually changed, with
one concrete sensory detail: the texture, the morning face,
one less step in the routine.

BEAT 4 · THE TRAIL OFF
"So yeah... I don't know, it just works for me."
Imperfect endings read as honest. A crisp call to action
would undo the whole clip.

      The master script prompt:

      Paste into Claude
      Write a spoken skincare review script in two parts for
[PRODUCT].

Part 1 (30 to 35 spoken words): the tired problem, then the
failed fix.
Part 2 (30 to 35 spoken words): the discovery of the
product, then a trail off close.

The failed fix is: [a real alternative her skin type would
have tried: highlighter, double cleansing, a viral serum,
drinking more water]

The concrete detail in the discovery is: [how it feels, what
changed in the mirror, which step it replaced]

Rules:
- Spoken language, contractions, one "like" or "I don't
  know" somewhere
- Banned words: radiant, luminous, transformative, game
  changer, holy grail, obsessed, glow up
- One idea per sentence
- She is talking to a friend, not presenting
- Part 2 ends mid thought, softly
- Only claims this product can genuinely support
      You now haveA two part review script with one real failed fix and one sensory detail.

      Cast

      The persona

      Generate a clean reference of the reviewer first: plain grey background, waist up, no makeup, natural skin with visible texture. That image is @image1, and whatever she wears in it she wears in both clips. A photo of the product on a real surface is @image2.

Woman (@image1), [AGE], [SPECIFIC FACE: skin tone, visible

She talks to the camera casually with small natural pauses,

"[PART 1 OR PART 2]"

Realism anchors: natural skin texture everywhere, no
smoothing, visible pores on the cheeks, tiny stray hairs at
the hairline, a real shadow from the window light, slightly
off centre framing, natural blinks, lip sync matched to
every word, a breath before the discovery line.
      You now haveA reference image per persona and a filled prompt for each part.
      Phase two · Render, check, test
      03
      Render

      Two clips, one voice

      1. Render Part 1 with no voice reference. The model gives her a voice.
      2. Extract the audio from the take you like and attach it as @audio1 on Part 2, so she sounds like one person.
      3. Render Part 2 from a slightly different position in the same room, with every description copied word for word from Part 1. Only the camera and the words change.
      4. Render one product B-roll: the product on the counter, window light moving across it, no hands. Render it once and reuse it in every persona's ad.

      In CapCut: Part 1, the B-roll over the join, then Part 2, with her voice running underneath the B-roll. Captions on, two to four words per line.

      05 · When it breaksTroubleshooting

      ProblemFix
      The skin looks airbrushedRepeat "visible pores, natural texture, no smoothing" and add "softness under the eyes kept visible".
      She sounds like a presenterRewrite with more fillers and a pause mid sentence. Shorten every sentence.
      The clip feels like an adCheck signals 2, 3 and 4: makeup, light, framing. One of them is too clean.
      The product label driftsSpell out the label text and the grip angle, and check every frame.
      Part 2 is a different womanThe locked blocks were reworded. Copy them from Part 1 word for word.
      The story feels genericThe failed fix is vague. Make it something her exact skin type would really have tried.
      A great hook and no salesThe discovery lacks its concrete detail. One sensory specific, not two vague ones.

      06 · AvoidWhat not to do

      • Don't smooth the skin. Ever. It is the one unforgivable tell in this category.
      • Don't put makeup on the reviewer. Production kills testimony.
      • Don't skip the failed fix. It is the beat that buys belief.
      • Don't use skincare marketing words. Nobody has ever said "radiant" in a real voice note.
      • Don't end on a call to action. The trail off is the close.
      • Don't run one persona. The cast is the system.
      • Don't over light. One window, one side, real shadows.
      • Don't make claims your product cannot support.
