      Worth reading anyway. Knowing what the workflow is doing is what lets you fix a clip that misses instead of re-rolling it.

      One good clip is easy now. A film is not. Same face, same place, same look, a story that climbs and an ending that lands, across clips that were each generated by a model that has never seen the others.

      Every AI film dies the same way. Scene one stuns, scene two drifts, scene three belongs to a different movie. Not because the renderer is weak. Because the model generating scene three has no memory of scenes one and two, and no way to infer anything you did not write down in front of it.

      The fix is not a better model. It is the order you work in. Story first, then the world, then the cast, then the scene map, and only then renders. By the time anything generates, every decision that could break the film has already been made in text, where changing your mind costs a retype.

      This works the same for a 30 second brand film and a 60 second short. The brand film simply has one extra rule: the product has to be the thing the story turns on.

      01 · Why it breaksEvery generation is a cold start

      The model has never seen your other clips. It does not know this is scene three. It cannot tell that a shirt was dry ten seconds ago. It has nothing except the reference images and the words in this one prompt.

      That produces two consequences. Almost everyone gets the second one wrong.

      Consequence one: nothing can be referenced. "The same corridor" builds a new corridor from scratch. "She still has the torch" invents a torch. "GENERATION 3 OF 4" is noise that implies a context which does not exist. And cumulative timecodes are the quiet killer. Writing SHOT 1 (00:45-00:47) tells a model with no memory to render the middle of something it cannot see.

      Consequence two: everything must be described, but everything can still change. The instinctive fix for consequence one is to freeze the world so nothing needs remembering. Same clothes, same place, same light, four times. That instinct kills the film. A story where nothing accumulates has no escalation, and viewers feel it straight away as "nothing is happening".

      The discipline is describe, don't infer. The world changes freely between scenes. Each prompt describes its own state completely, from scratch, in the present tense, as if the audience and the model are both seeing it for the first time.

      Depends on memory (breaks)Describes the state (renders)
      his shirt is now soaked and the maze is half destroyedhe wears an off-white cotton tee, soaked through and clinging to his chest and shoulders, the hem hanging heavy and dripping. Two of the three clotheslines are down, their sheets lying flat in the standing water; the third hangs intact.

      Same information. One needs a memory the model does not have. The other renders.

      The craft did not disappear with the camera crew. It moved to the decisions you make before the first render.

      The math

      A 60 second narrative short or a brand film the traditional way means director, crew, cast, locations, permits, a shoot day and a post schedule. Thousands at the very least, and weeks.

      This way: one operator, an afternoon of architecture, then a handful of generations. The architecture is the expensive part and it is all text. A bad story decision costs a retype instead of a reshoot. You can throw away three concepts before lunch, and that is the real leverage.

      05 · The workflowThe workflow, step by step

      Ten steps in three phases. Decide the film in words, lock the world, then render and finish. The first seven produce no video at all, and that is deliberate.

      Phase one · Decide the film in words
      01
      Story

      Style, spec and the spine gate

      Pick the visual style first: photoreal, live action with a film look, 2D anime, 3D, stop motion, found footage. It governs the concept, the failure modes, the reference images and the vocabulary in every brief. Never mix styles, or the model splits the difference and gives you neither.

      Then lock the spec: total duration, number of generations, length per generation, aspect ratio.

      Then run the spine gate. Five plain sentences, all five required:

      The spine gate
      WANT:        somebody wants something specific and concrete
OBSTACLE:    something or someone stands directly in the way
DECISION:    the character makes an active choice, not a discovery
CONSEQUENCE: the choice produces a physical result
CHANGE:      something is different at the end than at the start

      Read it aloud like a story someone told you. "A boy goes up to a roof to get his shirt, a man comes up behind him and cuts him off from the only door, he unhooks a clothesline and holds it across the aisle at chest height, the man runs into it, and the boy walks out past him." That is a film.

      Compare: "A boy on a roof notices the laundry moving strangely and finds his shirt cut in half." That has a Want and a Consequence and nothing else. It is an image, not a story, and an audience experiences it as nothing happening. The Decision line is the one most often missing.

      For a brand film, the same five sentences carry the ad.

      Spine lineIn a brand film
      WantWhat your customer wants, in their own world
      ObstacleThe thing in the way, which is the problem your product removes
      DecisionThe character chooses, and the product is what they choose or what makes the choice possible
      ConsequenceThe product does its physical job, on screen
      ChangeThe end state your customer is actually buying

      The first three seconds must raise a question the Want sets up. The last seconds are the Change, followed by the end card. One protagonist, one want, one location change at most in 30 seconds.

      You now haveA style, a locked spec and five sentences that pass the gate.
      02
      Story

      The four originality passes

      Pass 1: name the obvious version, then ban it. Write it down before designing anything, because unnamed defaults get built by accident. An anime short defaults to a schoolgirl on a rooftop with wind and blossom. A coffee brand film defaults to steam curling off a mug in golden light. Name yours and forbid it.

      Pass 2: move the camera to the wrong place. The person who arrives eleven minutes late. The person next to the event: the cleaner, the dispatcher, the kid in the back seat. One caution: that person still needs their own want, obstacle and decision, or a clever framing becomes a non-film.

      Pass 3: find the physical mechanism. Ask what physical fact about this world could decide the outcome. A rooftop hung with clotheslines at adult chest height, in a film about a child and an adult, holds its own ending: the child goes under without thinking, the adult does not. Plant it early with no emphasis and the ending feels inevitable instead of convenient.

      In a brand film, the product is the mechanism. Its real property decides how the story ends: the jacket that stays dry, the lock that holds, the bottle that stays cold. Plant it early and quietly, then let it decide the ending. The test is blunt: delete the product from the spine. If the film still works, the product was wallpaper.

      Pass 4: decide what stays unseen. Withhold the antagonist's motive, the moment of impact, what is in the bag. Never withhold the spine itself.

      You now haveThe default version named and banned, a physical mechanism planted, and a decision about what the audience never sees.
      03
      Story

      The pre-flight risk pass

      Before designing a single shot, list every element the concept needs and triage each one.

      • SAFE generates reliably. Build the film out of these.
      • CONVERT will not generate well as imagined. Redesign the beat so it delivers the same meaning another way.
      • HIDE must exist but must not be seen clearly. Make it partial, brief, blocked or under-lit.
      ElementTriageDecision
      Two adults fighting in clear viewCONVERTOne contact, not an exchange. Sustained fight choreography is where generation breaks.
      The moment of impactHIDECut on the contact frame. The impact is a cut, not an image.
      The antagonist's face across four clipsSAFE, with a conditionHe gets his own reference image. A silhouette costs clarity and drifts anyway.
      Laundry blowing in windCONVERTThe air is dead and the sheets are water heavy. Stated in every constants block.
      The product's logo, sharp and readableCONVERTThe shape, colour and material carry the product in the film. The logo lives on the end card.

      Look at what the triage did. It turned an unbuildable brief into a buildable film, and made it better. Constraints found early become the concept. Constraints found late become troubleshooting.

      Never reliable: readable text, logos, clocks with correct hands, readable screens, consistent reflections, and anything the audience can count. A wall of tally marks works because nobody counts them. A display reading "07" does not.

      You now haveEvery element of the concept marked SAFE, CONVERT or HIDE, with the redesigns written down.
      04
      Story

      The energy ladder

      Every scene gets a declared energy level, and the ladder climbs. A viewer should be able to tell which scene they are watching from its pulse alone, with the sound off.

      LevelCameraCut rateMotion in framePerformance
      1 StillLocked offOne shot per 3 to 4sOne element movesNeutral
      2 ObservedSlow deliberate movesOne shot per 3sSubject moves, world stillContained
      3 ActiveHandheld with weightOne shot per 2.5sSubject and one world elementUrgency
      4 PressedReal instability, whip pansOne shot per 2sSeveral elements displacedFull commitment
      5 BrokenViolent displacementOne shot per 1 to 1.5sThe environment is wreckedMaximum, then stillness

      The ladder must climb, and it may drop once after the peak. A 60 second film often runs 1, 3, 4, then 5 into 1. A 30 second brand film often runs 2, 4, then 1 on the Change. The drop to stillness is what makes an ending land, and it is where the product gets its clean moment.

      Energy is not a label. Writing "scene 3 is high energy" does nothing unless the prompt itself is different: camera behaviour, cut rate, how much moves in frame, and how hard the performance pushes. Same location, same light, same look, four dimensions changed.

      Shot budget: roughly one shot per 2.5 to 3 seconds, never more than six in a 15 second generation. Fewer, longer shots are almost always the more reliable and the more filmic choice.

      You now haveAn energy level for every scene, climbing, with a shot budget per generation.
      Phase two · Lock the world
      05
      World

      The World Bible, written as a constants block

      The film's look and physics, fixed in words, and pasted word for word at the top of every generation prompt. Identical wording is not a style preference. Two descriptions of the same thing produce two results, so "cold white torch" in one prompt and "harsh flashlight" in another are two different lights.

      The constants block template
      REFERENCE INPUTS: @image1 = [who/what, identity lock]. @image2 = [who/what,
  identity lock]. Both hold exactly as drawn in every shot.

[CHARACTER 1] (@image1): [age, build]. [Full wardrobe: every garment by colour,
  material, condition, how it is worn.] [Three identity anchors, named.]

[PRODUCT] (@image2): [shape, colour, material, finish, size in the hand.
  How it is held or where it sits.]

STYLE: [style, technique, grain, texture.]
WORLD RULES: [what exists in this world, and the explicit list of what never
  appears in it: no CG, no bloom, no clutter, no text in the world.]

LOCATION: [the space, its architecture, its geography. Where each feature sits
  relative to the others, by compass direction or a clear spatial relationship.
  Every surface and its condition.]

LIGHT: [the key source by name, its position, colour and hardness. Which parts
  of the space it reaches, and what the areas it does not reach look like.]

PALETTE AND GRADE: [3 to 4 colours that own the frame, the only saturated
  colours allowed, the same words every time.] Lens family: [one, held throughout].

MOTION RULE: [what does and does not move on its own, and the explicit statement
  that nothing moves unless something in frame moves it. Real weight, ground
  contact, natural cloth movement.]

Aspect ratio: [x]. Duration: [x] seconds.

      World Rules do the most creative work. A world defined only by what is in it looks generically epic. A world that also says what never appears in it looks like somebody's film.

      The geography line does the most technical work. Naming where the door sits relative to the light, and the light relative to the subject, is what lets separate generations feel like one continuous space. Without it, each generation invents its own floor plan.

      Light is a source, not a mood. "Cinematic lighting" is not a light. A sodium streetlamp above the door, amber, hard, is.

      Starters for four common brand looks are in the Templates section below.

      You now haveOne constants block, final, that you will paste unchanged into every prompt.
      06
      Cast

      The cast and the hero product

      Every character who appears in more than one generation gets their own numbered reference. So does the product in a brand film. No exceptions. Hiding a secondary character in silhouette to dodge drift fails twice: the film becomes hard to read, and the drift happens anyway.

      • Cast your customer. The protagonist of a brand film is the person you sell to, chosen on purpose. If the viewer cannot see themselves in the lead, the film is about somebody else.
      • Neutral light, neutral expression, plain background, always. A dramatically lit reference bakes its lighting into every generation. Flat and boring is correct.
      • Three distinct identity anchors per character: a scar through an eyebrow, a tape patch in a named position, a saturated colour accessory, a hair behaviour, a flaw in a garment. Match them to shot size. In a wide shot only high contrast shape and colour survive.
      • Full body or three quarter, so briefs can call any shot size later. A head and shoulders reference makes every wide shot a guess.
      • The product on a real surface, at a readable angle, in the same neutral light. Its silhouette, colour and material are what carry it through the film.
      • Every image looks like it came from the same production. Hold background tone, light direction, palette and crop identical.

      Typical counts: 15 to 20 second films need one or two images, 30 to 45 seconds need two to four, and a 60 second film with two characters and a product may need four or five. The full sheet prompts are in The Locked-Cast Production System, steps 02 to 04.

      Do not add a reference for progressive wear like wet clothing or dirt. References hold identity. The state ledger holds condition.

      You now haveA numbered set of neutral, matching reference images, generated and saved before any video render.
      07
      World

      The state ledger

      The constants block holds everything that never changes. The state ledger holds everything that does, written so it never depends on memory. One row per scene, and every cell is a complete description that stands on its own.

      SceneSubject stateLocation stateLight state
      1Off-white tee, dry and clean, collar stretched. Sandals dry.Three clotheslines strung intact, sheets hanging straight. Concrete wet with shallow standing water.Sodium lamp burning steadily, full amber.
      2Off-white tee damp across the shoulders and hem. Sandals wet, dark at the edges.Three lines intact. One sheet slipped a peg, hanging at an angle.Sodium lamp burning steadily, full amber.
      3Tee soaked through and clinging, hem dripping. Grit across one hip. Both palms red and raw.Two lines strung; the third lies flat in the standing water with its sheets.Sodium lamp flickering, dropping to half and recovering.
      4Tee soaked, one shoulder seam pulled open, forearms grazed.One line pulled tight across the aisle at chest height, bare. Fallen sheets in the water.Sodium lamp steady, full amber.

      Notice the light. Which lamp it is, and where it hangs, lives in the constants block and never changes. What it is doing in this scene lives here. That is how a world stays consistent and still escalates.

      How to check a row. Read the cell aloud with no other context. If it makes complete sense to someone who has seen nothing else, it is right. If it contains a comparison, a change verb or a time reference, it is wrong.

      You now haveA ledger row for every scene where each cell reads correctly on its own.

      08
      Plan

      The scene map

      The plan you work from, and the only place film time is allowed to appear anywhere in the whole deliverable.

      SceneFilm timeLengthStory beatEnergyOpens onCloses onImages
      100:00-00:1515sHe goes for his shirt; a man comes through the door1 StillLocked wide, doorwayThe door opening behind him@image1, @image2
      200:15-00:3015sThe man comes up the aisle; he runs; he is cornered3 ActiveGround macro, bootsPinned at the parapet@image1, @image2

      Then throw the film time away. It never enters a prompt.

      The seam rule: every scene opens on a different shot from the previous close. Different framing, different movement, different distance. Two independent generations never match a moving frame, and drift reads as a fault. A cut to a new angle is invisible because the eye expects cuts. Vary the seam type: a detail macro, a reverse angle, an environment plate, a point of view, a jump in scale.

      Inserts live at the seams. A brand film often needs one clean product moment the story generations should not have to carry: a macro of the mechanism working, the product at rest in the final stillness. Render it as its own short insert and place it at a seam, where a new angle is expected anyway. Add it to the map as its own row, marked INSERT. Inserts are the exception, not the method. Every extra clip is an extra seam.

      You now haveA scene map with a beat, an energy level, an opening and a closing shot for every generation, and any inserts placed at seams.
      09
      Render

      The generation prompts

      Each generation gets its own brief containing: its own duration and aspect ratio, its reference images by number, the constants block word for word, this scene's ledger row, this scene's energy level and what that means for camera, cut rate and motion, the story beat in one sentence, the opening shot and how it differs from the previous close, the shot budget, and the instruction that every timecode starts at 00:00.

      Strip from every prompt body: generation numbers, any instruction addressed to you instead of the model, score or music direction, any reference to another scene, any post production fallback, any take count.

      Sound. Seedance 2.0 generates audio in the same pass, so describe only sound with a visible cause in frame: footsteps on wet concrete, a door slamming, a single spoken line. That generated sound is your film's sound bed. Score direction inside a prompt either does nothing or bleeds into the picture. Music goes in during the finishing pass.

      Inserts use a shorter brief of the same kind:

      The insert template
      [16:9 or 9:16]. [4 to 6] seconds. One continuous shot. Timecodes start at 00:00.

[CONSTANTS BLOCK, word for word]

STATE: [the ledger row of the scene this insert sits next to, word for word]

ACTION: [one physical action, nothing else]
CAMERA: [framing, lens and one movement]
SOUND: [only sound with a visible cause in frame]

No text on screen. No music.

      Seedance 2.0 renders at least 4 seconds, so render inserts a little long and pick the moment in the finishing pass.

      10
      Finish

      The finishing pass

      The edit adds. It never repairs. That is law 6, and it is the difference between a film and a rescue job.

      1. The straight stitch test first. Drop every clip on the timeline in scene map order, end to end, no trims, no grade, no music. Watch it once. If it does not play as one film, stop. The fix is in the plan or the prompt, never on the timeline.
      2. Trim at the seams, on action. Cut into the next clip on a movement, not on a held frame. Pick the moment inside each insert.
      3. Score under the generated sound. The footsteps, the room and the cloth from the generations stay as the bed. Music sits underneath. Silence under a score is what reads as AI.
      4. One look pass across the whole timeline, if you want a look at all. Never clip by clip. If one clip needs its own correction to match, its constants block was not word identical: fix that prompt and re-render the clip.
      5. Titles and the end card. Text and logos never render reliably, so they live here, in brand type. The end card is the only place allowed to look like an ad.
      6. Cut the short versions. 6, 10 and 15 seconds for placements. If the 15 second version cannot tell the story, the spine was too complicated.
      You now haveA finished film with a score, an end card and its cutdowns, and not one repair on the timeline.

      07 · Quality gateThe banned words, the self-check and the five holds

      Inside a prompt body these words almost always signal an inference the model cannot make. When one appears, rewrite the sentence as a complete present tense description.

      TypeWords
      Change verbsnow, still, again, already, remains, continues, returns, becomes, no longer
      Comparativesmore, less, further, darker, wetter, worse, increasingly
      Referencessame as, like before, as established, from the previous, earlier
      Sequence markersscene 2, generation 3, of 4, continuing, next, following
      Meta instructionspaste this, the user should, in the edit, in post
      Hypestunning, breathtaking, epic, cinematic used as filler

      One legitimate exception: "the same X across every shot" inside a single prompt is an identity lock, and it is correct.

      Run this on every prompt before you render. It is mechanical on purpose. These failures survive being understood, which is why they must be checked instead of trusted.

      Then, on the straight stitch, audit what the viewer actually sees. A film is a system of five holds:

      HoldWhat the viewer checksWhere it comes from
      CharacterSame face, wardrobe and anchors in every clipReferences and the constants block
      LookSame palette, grain and lens feelPALETTE AND GRADE, word identical
      WorldSame geography and physicsLOCATION and MOTION RULE
      LightThe same source, doing what the ledger saysLIGHT plus the ledger
      StoryEvery clip moves the same spine forwardThe spine gate and the scene map

      The final test: does the straight stitch play as one film, and would it collapse if you removed the product? If the first answer is no, the plan is not finished. If the second is no, it is not a brand film yet.

      08 · When it breaksTroubleshooting

      ProblemFix
      Clips will not cut togetherThe constants block is not word identical. Paste, never paraphrase.
      The face changes between scenesA missing reference, or anchors too fine for the shot size. Use shape and colour for wide shots.
      The look shifts clip to clipPALETTE AND GRADE was reworded somewhere. Fix the prompt, not the grade.
      Everything looks generically epicYour World Rules are empty. Say what never appears in this world.
      The film feels flatYou froze the world. Build a state ledger and let it change.
      "Nothing happens"No spine. Go back to the five sentences and find the Decision.
      It is a nice film but not an adThe product is wallpaper. Rebuild pass 3 until the product decides the ending.
      A scene renders the wrong momentCumulative timecodes leaked in. Every prompt restarts at 00:00.
      Shots blended or droppedOver budget. Cap at one shot per 2.5 to 3 seconds.
      A visible seam between scenesTwo similar angles. Make the next opening shot as different as possible.
      The location changes shapeThe geography line is missing from the constants block. Name what sits where.
      Motion feels floatyThe MOTION RULE lacks physics: real weight, ground contact, natural cloth movement.
      Music bled into the pictureScore direction leaked into a prompt. Keep sound diegetic and add music in the finishing pass.
      Late scenes drift the mostFront load identity. Move to hands, backs and silhouettes as the film goes on.

      09 · AvoidWhat not to do

      • Don't render before the spine, the constants block, the references and the scene map exist. Rendering first is how films die.
      • Don't put film time in a prompt. If you write SHOT 1 (00:45-00:47), the prompt is broken.
      • Don't freeze the world to solve the memory problem. Describe the change fully instead.
      • Don't deliver atmosphere as a film. The spine gate is not optional.
      • Don't let the product sit in every frame. It enters where the story needs it and decides the ending.
      • Don't flatten the energy across scenes. The ladder climbs.
      • Don't plan around the edit. If a shot only works with compositing or matching, redesign the shot.
      • Don't overload a generation. Six shots maximum in 15 seconds.
      • Don't hide a recurring character to avoid drift. Give them a reference.
      • Don't run more than six generations. Past that, identity drift dominates. Restructure instead.
      • Don't stretch to 60 seconds because you can. The 15 or 30 second version is usually the better film and the better ad.

      10 · RhythmFrom one film to a campaign

      WeekActionOutput
      Week 1Spine, risk pass, constants block, references, ledgerThe world exists
      Week 2Scene map, every generation and insert, the straight stitch testThe footage exists
      Week 3Finishing pass and end card. Ship the hero film.The film is live
      Week 4Cut the 6, 10 and 15 second versions for placementsThe film becomes a campaign

      One world, built once, produces a hero film and a month of cutdowns. The next film in the same world starts at step 08.

      11 · StartYour first film this afternoon

      Install the skill, then give it one line about the film you want. For a brand film, put your customer and your product into that line. It runs everything above: picks the style, forces the spine through five sentences, bans the obvious version, finds the mechanism, triages every element, builds the ladder, the constants block and the ledger, casts the references and maps the scenes.

      You generate the reference images, then the clips, in order, checking each prompt against the gate before it renders. Then you finish it without repairing a thing.

      A film that needs troubleshooting was planned wrong. Everything on this page happens while redesign is still free, and that is the entire point.
