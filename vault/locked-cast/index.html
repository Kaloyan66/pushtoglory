The Locked-Cast Production System

One character, one product, fifty ads that all look like one brand. The asset setup and the Seedance 2.0 workflow that keeps the same face, wardrobe and light shot after shot.

      The same face, the same jacket and the same kitchen, shot after shot after shot. That is the wall everyone hits with AI video, and this is the setup that takes it down. No camera, no set, no crew. One operator and one locked production.

      Here is what changes. You stop prompting shot by shot and hoping. You stop watching the face change by the second shot. You stop burning credits re-rolling renders that were never going to match.

      You build the assets once, you lock them, and every ad you ship for the next six months inherits the same cast, the same product and the same look.

      01 · Why it breaksConsistency is a memory problem

      Models can render the same face beautifully. They just cannot remember it.

      Here is how almost every AI video workflow drifts:

      1. The character gets generated in one tool.
      2. The wardrobe gets described slightly differently in the next prompt.
      3. The location comes from a third tool with different lighting logic.
      4. Every shot prompt describes the character again from memory, a little differently each time.
      5. The model fills in the missing details, differently each time.
      6. By the second shot the face has changed, the jacket has new buttons and the kitchen has moved.

      The old way treats every shot as a new conversation. The fix is structural. One chat holds the whole production: script, character, wardrobe, locations, every shot prompt. Reference sheets are built once, and every shot inherits them instead of imagining them again.

      Most people fix drift downstream, re-rolling shots until one happens to match. Fix it upstream, in the assets, where every future shot inherits the work.

      02 · The rulesThe 7 drift points

      Every inconsistent AI video fails on one or more of these. Run every production against this table.

      #Drift pointWhat breaksThe fix
      1FaceA different jaw, eyes or skin between shotsA character sheet with a specific physical description, attached to every render
      2WardrobeButtons, colours and fit changeThe exact garment spec, restated word for word in every prompt
      3ProductLabel text and cap shape shiftA product sheet with the label spelled out, front and 3/4 views
      4Location and lightRooms remodel themselvesOne location sheet per set, with the light source and time of day fixed in words
      5GradeEvery shot colours itself differentlyThe palette named in the Production Doc, one grade pass in the edit
      6VoiceA different voice in every clipThe first approved voice reused as an audio reference on every clip after it
      7Story logicShots that do not cut togetherThe full shot list written before a single render, one action per shot

      Miss one and viewers feel it even when they cannot name it. Hit all seven and fifty ads read as one brand.

      The math

      The traditional route means crew, set, permits, casting and reshoots. Thousands per finished video, weeks per round, and the second video costs the same as the first.

      The locked cast route: one afternoon of asset setup, then every new ad reuses the same locked assets. Ad one carries the setup. Ad ten costs a script and some renders. Ad fifty costs the same as ad ten. Your time moves from managing renders to choosing angles, which is where it should have been all along.

      04 · The workflowThe workflow, step by step

      Ten steps in three phases. Build the cast once, shoot against it, then audit and ship. The first ad takes an afternoon. Every ad after it starts at step 05.

      Phase one · Build the cast once
      01
      Build

      Open the Production Doc

      Open one new Claude chat. This chat is your studio. Everything lives here and nothing leaves. Paste this and fill every bracket:

      Paste into a new Claude chat
      You are running a locked-cast AI video production for
[BRAND], which sells [PRODUCT].

Hold everything in this chat: the character bible, the
wardrobe spec, the locations, the script and every shot
prompt. Whenever I ask for a shot prompt, restate the
character, wardrobe, product and location anchors word for
word so nothing drifts between shots.

CHARACTER BIBLE
Name and archetype: [e.g. "Maya, 27, the honest best friend"]
Face: [face shape, skin tone, freckles or marks, eye colour,
brows, lips, nose. Be specific. Vague means drift.]
Hair: [colour, length, texture, parting, how it is worn]
Build and height: [build, height]
Energy: [how they move and talk]

WARDROBE SPEC
Outfit: [exact garments, fabric, colours, fit]
Accessories: [jewellery, watch, bag. Same in every shot.]

PRODUCT
[name, exact colours, material and finish, closure, and the
label text spelled out letter by letter]

LOCATIONS
Location 1: [room or place, light source, time of day]
Location 2: [same structure]

LOOK
Palette: [3 to 4 colours that own the frame]
Lens: [one lens feel, e.g. 35mm at eye level]

VOICE SPEC
[tone, pace, accent, energy. One voice for the whole
production.]

Confirm you have all of this locked, then wait for the script.

      Fill every bracket with specifics. "Dark hair" drifts. "Shoulder length dark brown hair with a centre part, tucked behind the left ear" locks.

      You now haveOne Claude chat holding the full bible, with Claude confirming it is locked.
      02
      Build

      The character sheet

      Two rules before you generate anything. Lock the character on a plain background first, because busy backgrounds are where the face drifts. And frame the sheet at 3/4 as well as front on, because the model holds identity better from an angle.

      Generate this in Higgsfield:

      Paste into Higgsfield image generation
      Character reference sheet on a plain warm grey studio
background. Two views side by side: front view and 3/4
left view, waist up.

[PASTE THE FULL CHARACTER BIBLE FROM THE PRODUCTION DOC]

Wearing: [PASTE THE WARDROBE SPEC]

Soft even studio lighting. Photorealistic. Natural skin
texture with visible pores, no smoothing. Sharp focus on
the face. Neutral expression. No props, no scenery,
nothing else in frame.

      Spend your credits here, not on re-rolling shots later. Generate until it is exactly your character. Fixing the sheet now is fixing every future shot at once. Save it. It becomes @image1 on every render.

      You now haveA character sheet you would happily see in fifty ads, saved as @image1.
      03
      Build

      The product sheet

      Paste into Higgsfield image generation
      Product reference sheet on a plain neutral background.
Two views side by side: straight on front view and 3/4
view.

Product: [PRODUCT with exact colours, the label text
spelled out, the cap or closure, material and finish]

Soft studio lighting, true to life colours,
photorealistic, sharp label detail. Nothing else in frame.

      The label is where product drift shows first. Spell the label text out in the prompt, and check it in every render.

      You now haveA product sheet with a clean, readable label, saved as @image2.
      04
      Build

      The location sheets

      One image per location, one angle each:

      Paste into Higgsfield image generation
      Location reference: [LOCATION from the Production Doc].
Empty scene, no people. [Light source and time of day].
Shot on a 35mm lens at eye level. Photorealistic, natural
shadows.
      You now haveOne empty, correctly lit plate per location, saved as @image3 and up.
      Phase two · Shoot against the locked cast
      05
      Shoot

      Script and shot list first

      Back in the Production Doc chat, ask for the script, then the full shot list, before a single render. Drift point 7 lives here.

      Paste into the Production Doc chat
      Write a [15/30] second ad script for [PRODUCT] from this
angle: [ANGLE].

Then build the shot list as a table:
| # | Beat | Action (one beat) | Camera | Location | Spoken line |

Rules: one action per shot. 3 to 5 seconds of screen time
per shot. Every shot serves the script. Mark which shots
show the product label.

      Read the table like an editor. Can you see the ad cut together? Fix it in the table, not in the renders.

      You now haveA script and a numbered shot list you can already picture as the finished ad.
      06
      Shoot

      Shot prompts that inherit everything

      Ask Claude for the shot prompts one at a time. Every prompt carries the anchors, word for word. This is the template:

      The shot prompt template
      Vertical 9:16. [4 to 8] seconds. One continuous shot.

REFERENCES: @image1 = the character sheet, identity lock.
@image2 = the product sheet. @image3 = the location plate.
@audio1 = the character's voice (from step 07 onward).

CHARACTER (@image1): [FULL character bible, word for word]
WEARING: [FULL wardrobe spec, word for word]
PRODUCT (@image2): [product spec, label text spelled out]
LOCATION (@image3): [location spec, light and time of day,
word for word]

ACTION: [one beat only]
CAMERA: [framing and movement, e.g. slow push-in from 3/4]
EMOTION: [one emotion]
SOUND: [room tone and any sound with a visible cause].
Spoken line: "[LINE]"

Hold the character's identity exactly as @image1. Natural
skin texture, no smoothing. The product label matches
@image2 exactly. No music, no captions, no text on screen.

      Notice what is not in it. No "shot 3 of 8". The model cannot see the other shots, so numbering only adds noise. The number lives in your shot list, not in the prompt.

      Render in Higgsfield with the sheets attached. One beat per shot gives you control: a bad shot costs one re-render instead of the whole ad. Seedance 2.0 renders at least 4 seconds, so render the beat a little long and trim it to the moment in the edit. Once a cast is stable you can also run two or three beats inside one 15 second generation.

      You now haveOne prompt per shot, every anchor pasted word for word, rendered in shot list order.
      07
      Shoot

      Lock the voice

      Seedance 2.0 generates the voice in the same pass as the picture. Left alone, every clip invents a slightly different one. Drift point 6.

      1. Render your first spoken shot without a voice reference. The model creates a voice for your character.
      2. When you have a take you like, extract its audio. That clip is now your voice reference.
      3. Attach it as @audio1 on every spoken shot after it, and keep the line "in the voice from @audio1" in the prompt.

      Prefer more control? Make the voice in ElevenLabs first, export a short sample, and use that as @audio1 from the very first shot.

      09
      Ship

      Assemble and test

      In CapCut: cut on action, not on shot ends. Run one grade pass across the whole ad so every shot shares the palette. Add captions, because most feeds play muted.

      Deploy three to five variations at equal budget. Review at 48 hours. These are starting thresholds, so calibrate them to your own account over time:

      MetricKill belowWatchScale above

      15 second hold10%10 to 18%18%
      Click through rate0.9%0.9 to 1.5%1.5%
      Cost per purchase vs target1.4x target1.0 to 1.4xBelow target

      Kill without mercy. A killed ad cost a retype, not a reshoot.

      You now haveThree to five live variations and a decision on each after 48 hours.
      10
      Ship

      The batch protocol

      Once the cast is locked, production becomes assembly.

      1. Keep one Production Doc per brand, forever.
      2. A new ad means a new script and new shot prompts in the same chat. The assets never change.
      3. Ship variations: same cast, different hooks, different locations, different angles.
      4. Every ad inherits the same face, wardrobe and grade, so the feed reads as one brand instead of fifty experiments.

      05 · TemplatesFour shots you will reuse in every ad

      Each one replaces the ACTION, CAMERA, EMOTION and SOUND lines of the shot prompt template. Everything else stays locked.

      The hook shot
      ACTION: [CHARACTER] turns to camera mid moment, as if
interrupted, and delivers the hook line
CAMERA: handheld feel, chest up, slight push-in
EMOTION: caught in the moment, direct
SOUND: room tone. Spoken line: "[HOOK]"
Length: 4 seconds
      The product demo
      ACTION: [CHARACTER] holds [PRODUCT] at chest level, label
to camera, a natural grip with real weight, tilting it
slightly while speaking
CAMERA: locked medium close-up
EMOTION: casual confidence
SOUND: room tone. Spoken line: "[LINE ABOUT THE PRODUCT]"
Length: 5 seconds
      The lifestyle beat
      ACTION: [CHARACTER] uses [PRODUCT] naturally in
[LOCATION], no eye contact with the camera
CAMERA: observational 3/4 angle, slow drift
EMOTION: unbothered, real life
SOUND: room tone and the sound of the product in use
Length: 4 seconds
      The end card
      ACTION: [PRODUCT] alone on [SURFACE] in [LOCATION], soft
light, a subtle shadow
CAMERA: slow push-in on the label
EMOTION: calm authority
SOUND: room tone only. The call to action goes on as text
in the edit.
Length: 4 seconds

      06 · When it breaksTroubleshooting

      ProblemFix
      The face shifts between shotsThe bible is too vague. Add face shape, skin markers and the exact hair. Re-render the sheet.
      The wardrobe changesRestate the full wardrobe spec in every shot prompt, not just the first.
      The product label is wrongSpell out the label text in the product sheet prompt and check it on every render.
      The location remodels itselfOne location sheet per set, attached to every shot in that location.
      The voice changes between clipsAttach @audio1 to every spoken shot and keep "in the voice from @audio1" in the prompt.
      Shots feel disconnectedYou skipped the shot list. Write every shot before you render any.
      The skin looks plasticAdd "natural skin texture, visible pores, no smoothing" to every prompt.
      Renders eat creditsYou are fixing downstream. Stop re-rolling and improve the sheets.

      07 · AvoidWhat not to do

      • Don't prompt a shot before the sheets exist. Every shot without a reference is a guess.
      • Don't describe the character from memory. Paste the bible word for word, every time.
      • Don't lock the character on a busy background. Plain background first, always.
      • Don't reference only a front view. 3/4 is where identity holds.
      • Don't re-roll a drifting shot more than twice. If it keeps drifting, the sheet is underspecified. Fix upstream.
      • Don't split the production across several chats. One chat, one production, one memory.
      • Don't let each shot grade itself. One grade pass in the edit.
      • Don't ship without the continuity audit. Viewers spot drift in half a second.
