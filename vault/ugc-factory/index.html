The AI UGC Factory System

One afternoon becomes a batch of ads. One winning angle becomes ten variations by Friday. The exact workflow for AI creator ads that look like a person, not an ad.

      People skip ads. They listen to people. A UGC ad works when it looks like somebody talking about something they liked, and this is the factory that produces that format on demand.

      The old way was a queue. Find creators, vet them, brief them, ship the product, wait, chase, and receive one usable video out of four. Negotiate usage rights. Start over for the next test.

      Every step of that queue existed because a human on camera was the only way to get the format. Remove the queue and what is left is the thing that actually wins in paid social: how fast you can test ideas. Twenty angles tested while a competitor is still scheduling one shoot.

      01 · Why it worksThe 9 native signals

      A viewer's brain files a clip under "person" or "ad" in the first second. These nine signals make that decision. Miss three and the spell breaks.

      #SignalWhat it doesCommon miss
      1Spoken languageContractions, fillers, one thought per sentenceMarketing copy read aloud
      2Phone framingSlightly off centre, eye level, closeCentred tripod symmetry
      3Window lightLight from one side, soft shadowsFlat, even studio light
      4Natural skinVisible texture and pores, no smoothingPlastic AI skin, the number one tell
      5A lived in backgroundA real room, slightly blurred, imperfectAn empty studio or a dream kitchen
      6Imperfect deliveryPauses, trailing off, a small laughFlawless newsreader pacing
      7Real product handlingWeight in the hand, the label at an angleA floating or pasted on product
      8One specific detailA concrete moment or numberVague praise like "so good"
      9A soft closeRecommendation energy, not a pitchA hard call to action in the last second

      The math

      The creator route: hundreds per video, two to seven days per round of briefs and revisions, one angle per spend, usage rights on top, and reshoot risk every time.

      The factory route: an afternoon per batch and every angle you can script. When a video flops, it cost a retype, not a re-brief. The bottleneck moves from production to judgment, which is exactly where you want it.

      03 · The workflowThe workflow, step by step

      Six steps. Research, script, cast, render, assemble, test. The first batch takes an afternoon. From the second batch on, you start at step 02 with angles you already mined.

      Phase one · Research and script
      01
      Research

      Mine the angles

      Do not write scripts first. Mine angles first. Paste 20 to 50 real customer reviews into a Claude chat. Yours, or a competitor's if you are launching.

      Paste into a new Claude chat, then paste the reviews
      Here are customer reviews for [PRODUCT].

Extract from them:
1. The 5 strongest PAIN angles: what life looked like before
   the product, in the customers' own words
2. The 5 strongest OUTCOME angles: what specifically changed
   after
3. The 3 most repeated OBJECTIONS: the doubts people had
   before buying
4. The 10 most vivid SPECIFIC details: phrases, moments and
   numbers worth stealing

Rank each list by how often the theme appears. Keep the
customers' actual wording wherever possible.

      Real ads are built from real language. This is where authenticity actually comes from, and it is the step almost everyone skips.

      You now haveRanked lists of pains, outcomes, objections and vivid details, in your customers' words.
      02
      Script

      The four beat script, in two parts

      Every script follows the same four beats: hook, story, turn, close.

      Seedance 2.0 renders up to 15 seconds per generation, so a 30 second ad is two clips. Write the script already split, so each part fits one generation with room to breathe. About 30 to 35 spoken words per part is 12 to 14 seconds.

      PartBeatsWhat happens
      Part 1Hook, then storyStop the scroll with one casual line, then the before state, told like a friend, with one specific failed alternative
      Part 2Turn, then closeThe product enters with one concrete detail, then a soft recommendation that trails off
      Paste into the same chat
      Write a UGC script for [PRODUCT] from this angle: [ANGLE].

Write it in two parts.
Part 1 is the HOOK and the STORY. 30 to 35 spoken words.
Part 2 is the TURN and the CLOSE. 30 to 35 spoken words.

Rules:
- Spoken language, contractions, one "like" or "honestly"
  somewhere
- One idea per sentence
- The hook uses this pattern: [PATTERN FROM THE LIST BELOW]
- The story includes this failed alternative: [FROM STEP 01]
- The turn includes this specific detail: [FROM STEP 01]
- No brand speak: never "game changer", "obsessed",
  "holy grail"
- The close trails off naturally. Recommendation energy,
  zero pitch.

Then write 2 alternative hooks that fit the start of Part 1
without changing the rest of it.

      The five hook patterns

      1. The confession: "I did not want to post this, but..."
      2. The compliment: "Three people asked me about this today."
      3. The mistake: "I was doing [routine] wrong for years."
      4. The skeptic: "I did not believe the reviews. I get it now."
      5. The countdown: "Week four. Here is what actually changed."
      You now haveA two part script and three hooks that all lead into the same story.
      Phase two · Cast and render
      03
      Cast

      Cast the creator

      Decide per brand. One consistent face builds recognition, and you build it properly in The Locked-Cast Production System. Rotating faces give you more ways to be trusted and better tests: different ages, skin types and energy.

      Generate a clean reference of each creator first, on a plain grey background, waist up, with natural skin texture. Whatever they wear in that image is what they wear in every clip, so pick an outfit you are happy to see all week. That image is @image1. A product photo at a readable angle, on a real surface rather than a white cutout, is @image2.

      Then the talking head prompt:

      The casting prompt for Seedance 2.0 in Higgsfield
      Vertical 9:16 UGC talking head video, about [12] seconds,
one continuous take, no cuts, no text on screen.

Person (@image1): [AGE] [GENDER], [SPECIFIC face: skin tone,
visible skin texture, freckles or marks, eye colour],
[HAIR: colour, length, how it is worn], [MAKEUP: none or
minimal]. Wearing [CASUAL SPECIFIC GARMENT].

Product (@image2): [PRODUCT, colours, the label spelled out],
held naturally with real weight.

Setting: [bedroom / kitchen / car / desk], natural light
from the [LEFT/RIGHT], the background real and slightly
blurred, lived in details visible.

Framing: phone video feel, eye level, slightly off centre,
close enough to feel personal.

They talk to the camera with casual energy, natural pauses
and a small smile where it fits. Spoken words:
"[PART 1 OR PART 2 OF THE SCRIPT]"

Realism anchors: visible pores and natural skin texture, no
smoothing, natural hand movement with real product weight,
imperfect framing, soft window shadows, lip sync matched to
every word with natural breaths.

      The imperfections are the point. Perfect light reads as an ad. Window light and an off centre frame read as a person.

      You now haveA saved reference for each creator and a filled casting prompt per script part.
      04
      Render

      Render the two clips and the B-roll

      1. Render Part 1 first, with no voice reference. The model creates a voice for the creator.
      2. Lock the voice. Extract the audio from the take you like and attach it as @audio1 on Part 2, so the creator sounds like one person across both clips.
      3. Render Part 2 from a different camera position in the same room. A new angle makes the join read as a camera change instead of a glitch.
      4. Render two or three B-roll inserts of the product alone: the same light, the same grade, a slow camera move, no hands.

      Everything in Part 2 that describes the person, the outfit, the product and the light must be copied word for word from Part 1. Only the camera position and the spoken words change. Any rewording reads as a different person or a different room.

      Phase three · Assemble and test
      05
      Assemble

      The batch matrix

      One afternoon of production runs the matrix:

      Hook AHook BHook C
      Creator 1Ad 1Ad 2Ad 3
      Creator 2Ad 4Ad 5Ad 6

      Here is why the two part script matters. The hook lives in Part 1, so Part 1 is rendered once per hook. Part 2 is rendered once per creator and reused under all three hooks. That is six ads from eight renders instead of twelve.

      In CapCut, order each ad as Part 1, then a B-roll insert, then Part 2. Drop the B-roll where the creator moves a hand or shifts position, and keep the voice running underneath it. The audio never stops, which is what makes two generations feel like one video. Then add captions: two to four words per line, centred, high contrast, clear of the platform buttons.

      When one ad wins, rebuild the matrix around it: same hook, new stories, new faces. Ten variations by Friday.

      06
      Test

      Kill or scale

      Deploy the batch at equal budget to the same audience. Review at 48 hours. These are starting thresholds, so calibrate them to your own account over time:

      MetricKill belowWatchScale above
      3 second hook rate25%25 to 35%35%
      15 second hold10%10 to 18%18%
      Click through rate0.9%0.9 to 1.5%1.5%
      Cost per purchase vs target1.4x target1.0 to 1.4xBelow target

      Then let Claude read the numbers with you:

      Paste into the same chat with your results
      Run a kill or scale review on this UGC batch.

Data per ad: [name, hook pattern, creator, spend, hook rate,
hold rate, click through rate, cost per purchase]

For each ad return:
1. Decision: KILL / HOLD / SCALE
2. The specific threshold that decided it
3. If SCALE: which element is winning (hook, creator or
   angle) and the next 3 variations to build on it
4. If KILL: which of the 9 native signals it most likely
   missed

Then the pattern call: which hook pattern and creator
combination should lead the next batch. Decisive calls, no
hedging.
      You now haveA decision on every ad and a brief for the next batch.

      06 · When it breaksTroubleshooting

      ProblemFix
      The skin looks plasticAdd "visible pores, natural skin texture, no smoothing" and repeat it in the prompt.
      The delivery sounds roboticWrite fillers and pauses into the script itself.
      The product looks pasted onDescribe the grip, the weight and the label angle in the prompt.
      Part 2 looks like a different personThe locked blocks were reworded. Copy them from Part 1 word for word and change only the camera and the words.
      The voice changes between partsAttach the Part 1 audio as @audio1 on Part 2.
      The clip feels like an adRun the 9 native signals and fix the weakest two.
      Hook rate is fine, clicks are deadThe picture works and the script does not. Tighten the turn.
      Every variant performs the sameYour hooks are too similar. Pull from three different patterns.

      07 · AvoidWhat not to do

      • Don't write scripts before mining reviews. Generic in, generic out.
      • Don't polish. Studio light, perfect skin and newsreader pacing are the three fastest ways to look like an ad.
      • Don't centre the subject. Real phone videos are slightly off.
      • Don't use marketing words. "Obsessed", "game changer" and "holy grail" are ad language.
      • Don't try to fit a 30 second script into one generation. Two parts, one join, hidden by B-roll.
      • Don't ship one video per angle. Single ads teach you nothing. The matrix exists for a reason.
      • Don't keep losers alive out of attachment. They cost a retype.
      • Don't run only UGC. Rotate in statics and cinematic formats so the feed never fatigues.
      • Don't skip captions. Most feeds play muted.
      • Don't present an AI creator as a real customer. Build every script from real reviews, make only claims the product can back, and follow your ad platform's rules on labelling AI generated people.

      09 · StartYour first batch

      Paste the mining prompt with real reviews. Pick the strongest angle. Script it in two parts. Cast two creators. Render the matrix. Deploy six, kill four, scale two, rebuild.

      By week four your account is full of ads that look like people, test like a lab and cost like software. Every winning ad starts with research, every variation is a data point, and every angle that works compounds.
