      Every marketer researches a niche the same way. Open the Ad Library, search a competitor you already know, scroll, screenshot, paste it into a doc, open forty tabs, lose half of them, and pick the angle on gut feel.

      The problem is not effort. Manual research simply does not scale. You cannot watch two thousand ads with your eyes. You cannot sort them by how long they have been running. And you cannot see which angle is missing from a niche unless you have seen every angle that is already there.

      You are not short on creativity. You are short on the full list of what already works, and a map of what nobody is doing.

      01 · The basicsMCP is the USB-C port for AI

      Model Context Protocol, MCP, is a standard that lets Claude plug into real software. Instead of guessing from memory, it calls real functions against real data. You connect a tool once, and from then on Claude decides which of its functions to use, with which filters, and in what order.

      Here is what actually changes. The connector gives Claude a readable list of what the tool can do, what each function accepts and what it returns. So you write in plain words, "find every active long running ad for water filters in the US and group them by angle", and Claude picks the calls, runs them in sequence and hands back the processed result. You write the brief. It writes the queries. No code, nothing to install beyond the connector.

      What a connector gives ClaudeWhat that means for you
      ToolsSpecific functions like searching ads, pulling a brand, transcribing a video. Claude sees the list and knows what each takes.
      Live dataNot what the model remembers from its training. What is running today.
      ActionsA connector can also do things: generate an image, start tracking a brand, upload a creative to an ad account.

      Every new connector is a new capability, without new software and without a new hire.

      02 · The distinctionMCP or API: the same door, opened differently

      This gets explained wrong constantly, so it is worth being precise. An API does not need a programmer any more. In Claude Code or Cowork you describe the result you want and Claude writes a working script that talks to the API directly. You do not need to code. You need to describe the outcome.

      So what is the real difference? MCP is easier. The API is more powerful. One is a menu, the other is the kitchen.

      MCP, for askingAPI, for running
      Nothing to set up beyond connecting onceNo ceiling on volume: 20,000 rows take the same effort as 200
      Claude chooses the tools and the orderThe same output format every time: CSV, database, dashboard
      Ideal for research, when you do not know in advance what you will needRuns on a schedule, with no chat open and nobody at the screen
      Each step builds on the last, in one conversationReaches everything in the documentation, not only what the connector exposes
      Limited to what the connector's author exposedNeeds a script written once, which Claude writes for you

      The argument that settles it: everything that goes through a connector also goes through the model's context. Two hundred ads in a conversation are comfortable. Twenty thousand rows are slow, expensive and often simply do not fit. A script against the API writes them to a file, and Claude only reads the summary.

      The rule: do it once, use MCP. Do it every day, use the API. And when a connector does not cover something, the API almost always does. A common example: an ads connector reads campaigns, creatives and insights, but not the comments under your ads. That does not mean it cannot be done. It means that job is a small script against the platform's API, which is exactly what section 08 uses.

      The math

      A manual niche teardown is weeks of scrolling, screenshots and unwatched videos, and it still ends in an opinion. Then comes the brief, the production, the edit and the resize, which is more weeks.

      Connected: research, angle, script, image, video and every format in one conversation, in an afternoon. You find out an angle is taken before you spend anything on it, instead of after.

      04 · The workflowThe teardown, move by move

      Eight moves. Each is one request, and each builds on the result of the one before, which is why it all happens in one conversation instead of five tools. The example running through it is a real kind of niche: countertop and tap water filters.

      Phase one · Map the market
      01
      Scout

      Pick the niche

      The first request is cheap and takes seconds. It returns the whole category list with how many brands advertise in each.

      Read the count as a signal, not a score. Many brands means proven demand, and expensive auctions. Few brands means cheap traffic, and possibly no market. You are not looking for an empty niche. You are looking for a proven niche with an uncovered angle, or an uncovered market.

      Do not just look at the biggest number. Look at brands against markets. A category with thousands of advertisers sounds impossible until you see that nearly all of them target one country. For your own country or region, the same niche can be practically empty.

      Paste into Claude
      Show me every niche in the ad library and how many brands
advertise in each.

Then flag the niches with a high brand count that is
concentrated in one or two countries, and tell me which of
them have little or no activity in [YOUR MARKET].
      You now haveA shortlist of niches with proven demand and room to enter.
      02
      Scout

      Map the players

      Search a competitor by name and you see straight away what manual digging almost always misses: big brands do not advertise from one page. A category leader often runs a separate ad account for every market. Look at only one and you underestimate them several times over.

      Three things to read from one search:

      • The same domain under two records is one operator. Add them together, do not read them separately.
      • A leader showing up in a small market is a signal, not a coincidence. They have already tested that the model works there.
      • Check who is actually in the auction. The biggest brands in a category by revenue are often nearly absent from Meta. The real fight may be a race between a handful of direct to consumer brands, and that changes who you position against.
      Paste into the same chat
      Search for [COMPETITOR] and every brand record connected to
it. Group the records by operator (same domain or clearly the
same company), and for each give me: active ad count, the
country each account targets, and the landing domain.

Then list the 10 brands with the most active ads in this
niche, and tell me which well known brands in the category
barely advertise here at all.
      You now haveEvery brand actually spending in the niche, grouped by operator, with its markets.
      03
      Filter

      Pull only the survivors

      This is the real difference between scrolling and querying. Nobody keeps paying for months behind an ad that loses money. How long an ad has been running is the most honest signal you have, and you filter by it before you read a single line of copy.

      FilterSettingWhy
      StatusActive onlyWhat runs now, not what ran once
      Days running60 or moreNothing survives two months unless it pays for itself
      Performance tierThe top tier onlyA second opinion on persistence and reach
      Ads per brandTwo at mostSo one heavy spender does not eat the whole list
      Country and languageYour marketShows whether anyone advertises in your language at all
      Landing page typeProduct page, advertorial, listicle, quiz, VSLYou copy the funnel structure, not just the ad
      Read the scores correctly

      Performance tiers in ad libraries are modelled estimates of how long and how widely a creative is being run. They are not ROAS and they are not confirmed spend. Read them as "worth looking at", never as "this is winning".

      You now haveA list of long running ads, balanced across brands, in the markets you care about.
      Phase two · Find the gap
      04
      Break down

      Take every ad apart

      For each ad that survived the filter, the library returns the full copy, the headline, the landing page, the CTA, the format, the market and language, how long it has run, a transcript of the video, and still frames from the opening seconds.

      Those opening frames are the most underrated thing in the whole stack. The frames at 0, 1 and 3 seconds are, quite literally, your competitor's hook, handed to you as images.

      Break every ad into the same fields, so two hundred ads can actually be compared:

      FieldWhat to capture
      HookThe first three seconds, in words and in the frame
      Angle structureThe order of the argument, e.g. story, symptom, investigation, mechanism, proof
      Proof typeTestimonial, demonstration, published testing, numbers, authority
      OfferDiscount, bundle, guarantee, or none at all
      CTA and landing pageWhere it sends people, and what kind of page that is
      FormatStatic, video length, carousel, dynamic creative

      One pattern to look for. In the water filter niche, one of the most persistent ads runs for weeks with no discount at all. A personal story, a symptom, an investigation, the mechanism, then published lab testing as proof. In a category where almost everyone fights with a percentage off, that ad sells with a story and transparency. That tells you the angle is carrying the result, not the offer. And an angle like that travels to another market without eating your margin.

      You now haveEvery long running ad reduced to the same six fields.
      05
      Map

      Build the angle map

      One breakdown is interesting. The value arrives when the same thing runs across the whole niche and Claude returns a map instead of a list.

      Group angles by the psychological lever they pull, not by the product. That way you see immediately which lever is overloaded and which one nobody is using. For water filters, the map looks like this:

      LeverAngles inside itState in this niche
      A · Fear, the invisible threatNamed contaminants, the shock statistic, "meeting regulations is not the same as safe"The number one lever, already overloaded
      B · Fear, urgency and survivalWhen the water stops, independence from the grid, being readyIts own category with its own emotional register
      C · Curiosity, the open loop"Does filtering even matter?", "what is your tap's score?", "the best filter of the year"Cheap clicks from cold traffic, barely used
      D · Desire, sensory and beautyTaste transformation, skin and hairConverts without fear, and it is open
      E · Identity, one specific personParents, a new home, homeowners protecting appliancesTalks to someone, not to everyone
      F · Mechanism and product truthCustomised to your water, keeps the minerals, no installation, premium materialDemonstration creative, where products really differ

      Then build the ownership matrix. Owns means a brand has built its identity on that angle. Uses means it runs the angle but does not own it.

      AngleBrand ABrand BBrand CBrand DBrand E
      Named contaminantsusesusesOWNSusesuses
      Regulations are not safety···OWNS·

      That table is the entire teardown in one picture. Every owned row tells you where not to fight head on. Every empty row tells you where there is space. And when most of the angles in a niche are fear, you learn something else: the audience is already trained to scroll past the next scary ad. Angle fatigue is as real as creative fatigue.

      Finally, place every brand on two axes: what is at stake (an everyday upgrade, or a necessity) and how the ad talks (fear of what is wrong, or a lifestyle and sensory gain).

      FearLifestyle

      06
      Choose

      Take the empty angle

      Between the map and production sits the one thing Claude does not decide for you: which empty space is yours.

      Ask for angles that come directly from holes in the matrix. They are not brainstormed ideas. They are the difference between what exists and what is possible. Six of twelve from the water filter teardown:

      #AngleLeverWho it is forDraft hook
      1The formula waterFear of a new parentNew parents, who buy easily"You would never mix the bottle with dirty water. So why does it come from the same tap?"
      2Your coffee tastes like the pipesEnthusiast, tasteCoffee and tea lovers, high order value"A premium espresso machine and chlorinated tap water. You are tasting the plumbing, not the beans."
      3What is really in your city's water?Hyperlocal curiosityCold traffic, targeted by city"Type your city. See what comes out of your tap. Most people are surprised."
      4The bottled water billRational price, anti plasticPrice watchers and eco buyers"$4 a day on bottles is $1,460 a year."
      5The renter's filterRenter identity, no installationRenters, a huge and ignored group"Renting? You cannot change the pipes. You can stop drinking from them."
      6The dog drinks it tooPet parentPet owners"You filter your own water, then fill the dog's bowl from the tap."

      Know the closed doors too. Pure contaminant fear is taken by several brands, so you would be the fourth identical voice. A whole home "free water test, then a quote" model needs installers and territory, not just ads. The map is worth exactly as much as it tells you where not to spend.

      A free angle you cannot deliver is a trap, not an opportunity. Before you commit, check it against your product, your margin and your delivery.

      You now haveOne chosen angle, with its lever, its audience, a draft hook and the reason it is free.
      Phase three · Produce and automate
      07
      Produce

      Brief the creative from the teardown

      Up to here everything was research. Now the direction flips. The same conversation that found the angle already holds the brief: the hook, the tone, the format, the landing page, even how long the competitor's videos run. Production does not start from a blank page. It starts from a finished brief.

      FromToWith
      Angle and hookScript and storyboardClaude
      ScriptImages and variationsHiggsfield or Magnific
      ImagesVideo, avatar and voiceHiggsfield or Magnific
      One creative1:1, 4:5 and 9:16Auto resize in the production tool
      Finished formatsA live campaignThe Meta Ads connector

      Here is angle 2 taken all the way to a static ad. The brand is invented for the example.

      The RAW / UGC photo prompt
      Photo taken on a [CAMERA BODY], [LENS] at f/2.2, ISO 200.
A home espresso machine on a slightly cluttered kitchen
counter, a chrome tap behind it with visible limescale
around the base, a few coffee grounds and crumbs on the
worktop.

Light: morning sun through a window on the left, uneven,
one side of the counter overexposed, a hard edged
shadow from the tap.

A hand at the edge of frame, slightly motion blurred,
reaching for the cup. Crooked framing, like a phone photo
taken in a hurry.

Do not beautify. Do not smooth surfaces. Do not retouch.
Negative: showroom kitchen, perfect symmetry, catalogue
styling, glossy CGI, stock photo.
      The ad copy
      A premium espresso machine and chlorinated tap water.
You are tasting the plumbing, not the beans.

The tap filter takes out the chlorine and the limescale
in a 30 second fit, and your coffee finally tastes like
the beans you paid for.

Headline: Your coffee tastes like the pipes
CTA: Shop now

      Why it does not look like AI: because it is not pretty. A used counter, uneven light, a blurred hand, a crooked frame. The average AI image is clean and symmetrical, and that is exactly what gives it away. Ask for two variations in the same request and pick one instead of re-rolling.

      The line you never cross: replicate the structure, never the assets. Take the logic of the hook, the rhythm, the order of the argument and the type of proof, and fill them with your product, your faces, your voice and your claims. Never take someone else's video, photos, logo or reviews. Three reasons, in this order: legal risk, the risk of losing the ad account, and the most practical one. Copied creative is already burned. That audience has seen it. You win on the uncovered angle, not the copied one.

      08
      Automate

      Skills and scheduled tasks

      Everything so far happened because you sat down and typed a request. A scheduled task removes you from the equation: an instruction plus a schedule, and the work happens without you starting it. The difference between a tool and a system is the schedule.

      The one thing to understand about the mechanics: every scheduled run starts a brand new session with no memory of the last one. It does not remember yesterday's report, your preferences or your product's name. So the instruction has to stand on its own: where the data comes from, the criteria for filtering it, the format to arrange it in, and where to leave the result.

      What one automated ad account can look like:

      WhenTaskOutput
      07:00 dailyWhat competitors launched in the last 24 hours, only ads already climbingFive lines instead of a quarterly deep dive
      08:00 dailyYesterday's ads and sales in one reportSpend, ROAS and CPA against last week, before you open the laptop
      Every 3 hoursNew comments under active ads, classifiedRepeat questions answered in your voice, complaints sent to a person
      21:00 dailySpend for the dayNo surprises in the morning
      MondayCreative fatigueThe three ads to replace this week, and which angle from your map replaces them
      FridayThe client report, if you are an agencySame format every week, saved to Drive, ready to review rather than write

      The comments task is the most underrated of them. A comment like "this is a scam, I waited three weeks" sitting unanswered under your most expensive ad drags its click through rate down more than any hook pulls it up.

      A good scheduled instruction looks like this. Notice it gives thresholds, a baseline for comparison, a format and a destination:

      Schedule: every day at 08:00
      Pull yesterday's data from the ad account and from the store.

For every campaign that spent more than [$X]: spend,
purchases, revenue, ROAS and CPA, and the same numbers for
the same day last week.

Flag in red every ad set that spent more than [$Y] with no
purchase. Flag in yellow every creative whose click through
rate has dropped more than [Z%] against its own first week.

Finish with three sentences on what happened and one
sentence on what needs a human decision today.

Format: a short table plus the summary. No explanation of
the method. Post it to the [CHANNEL] channel and save a
copy to Drive.

      "Check how the ads are doing" would return vague commentary every morning. This returns the same decision, made by the same criteria, every day, which is the entire point.

      The task says what. The skill says how. A skill is a folder of instructions, and files when needed, that a task runs through every time. Because each run has no memory, skills are not a convenience here. They are the condition. Without them you get a slightly different tone, a different format and, worst of all, different claims about your product every day.

      SkillWhat it holds
      brand-voiceFormal or casual, banned words, how the product is written about, what you never claim, emoji or none
      product-dataSpecs, sizes, ingredients, prices, delivery times, warranty, the most common questions. Stops the most expensive mistake: an invented feature in an ad or a reply.
      people-realismThe method for images that do not look AI: camera, light, named imperfections, negative prompts
      report-formatThe exact columns, thresholds, section order and length, so Monday's report compares to Friday's
      comment-rulesWhat gets answered automatically and what gets escalated, with templates for delivery, price, size and stock
      niche-teardownThe structure of this page as a skill: which requests, in what order, which matrix, what output. The next niche becomes one request instead of ten.

      A prompt works once and disappears into a chat history. A skill improves. Every time you find what gives an AI image away, or which word in a comment reply was wrong, you fix it once and every future task gets better. That is where your real advantage lives. Not in access to the tools, which everyone has.

      The boundaries:

      BoundaryRule
      MoneyRead-only tasks run freely. A task that raises a budget or pauses a campaign runs only with clear thresholds and a person who confirms. One bad day is not a trend.
      VoiceAutomatic replies only for clear, repeated questions. Complaints, health claims, disputes and anything emotional always go to a person.
      MemoryEvery run is the first run. If an instruction relies on "like last time", you get a different result every morning.
      CheckingRead every result by hand for the first five to seven days before you trust it. You will find a wrong threshold or an awkward format, and you fix it once.
      You now haveOne read-only scheduled task running, backed by a skill, and read by hand for its first week.

      05 · SetupConnect it in fifteen minutes

      This is the part most marketers are afraid of, for no reason. Connecting a tool is like adding an app: paste an address and a key, approve the access, done.

      1. Install the Claude desktop app. Claude Code and Cowork work too. For a marketer the desktop app is the shortest path, with no terminal.
      2. Get an API key from the ad library tool, usually under Settings, then API. The same goes for your production tool. The key is the password Claude uses to act on your account, so treat it like one and never post it anywhere.
      3. Add the connector in Claude under Settings, Connectors. Paste the MCP server address and the key. The tool's functions appear in the list straight away.
      4. Approve only what you use. Allow read-only tools freely. For anything that spends credits or launches campaigns, keep confirmation on for every call until you are used to it.
      5. Run one free test request, such as the niche list from move 01. If it answers, the connection works.
      6. Write your standard as skills: brand voice, product data, report format, the realism method. Written once, used by everyone, and every later task runs through them.
      7. Schedule what repeats. Start with one read-only task and read it by hand for a week before you add a second.

      The rule for adding connectors: connect a tool when your question repeats. A one-off lookup does not justify a new connector. "Every morning I want to know X" justifies it immediately.

      06 · BriefsThe four you will use 90% of the time

      Notice what they share. They say what result you want and by which criteria. They never say which tool to call, or with which filters. That is Claude's job.

      01 · The niche teardown
      Do a teardown of the [PRODUCT] niche in [MARKET].

Pull every brand advertising actively, with its number of
ads. For each brand give me: the product, the formats, its
top angles with the real lines from the ads, the offer
model and the landing page.

Pay attention to which ads have run for more than 60 days.

Finish with a table showing which brand OWNS each angle and
which brands only USE it.
      02 · Breaking down one competitor
      Take [BRAND]. Give me its top 10 ads by days running.

For each one: the hook in the first 3 seconds, the copy
structure, the type of proof, the offer, the CTA and the
landing page type. Transcribe the videos.

Then tell me the three things they repeat in EVERY ad.
That is their formula.
      03 · Finding the empty space
      Based on everything so far: which psychological levers are
overloaded in this niche, and which are free?

Give me 12 angles that NOBODY is running, each with: the
lever, the target audience, a draft hook, and why it is free.

Leave out any angle my product cannot serve: [WHAT YOUR
PRODUCT CAN AND CANNOT HONESTLY CLAIM].
      04 · Production
      Take angle number 3. Write a 30 second UGC script in the
structure that works for [BRAND]:
problem, personal story, mechanism, proof, CTA.

Then generate 4 versions of the opening frame and export
them in 1:1, 4:5 and 9:16.

      The rule for a good brief: give a criterion ("running more than 60 days"), a format ("a table of who owns which angle") and a constraint ("leave out angles I cannot serve"). A prompt without a criterion returns commentary. A prompt with one returns a decision.

      07 · TrapsWhat the tools will not do for you

      The tool is fast. The judgement is still yours.

      TrapWhat to do instead
      Scores are not ROAS. "Top tier" means a brand runs a creative persistently and widely, not that it makes money. Nobody publishes their ROAS.Use the tier to decide what to look at, never as proof something wins.
      The data is a snapshot. Active ad counts change several times a day.Pull the numbers again right before you make a decision on them.
      Coverage has gaps. Country filters cover some markets better than others. An empty result for your country can mean "not covered", not "nobody advertises".Check with a second source, such as the public Meta Ad Library, before calling a market empty.
      The angle is still your choice. Claude gives you the full list and the empty spaces.Which space is yours depends on your product, margin and delivery.

      The most common mistake is asking "what is the best angle?" and accepting the answer. The right question is "which angles are taken, by whom, and how strongly?" That one is factual and checkable. The strategic call stays with you.

      08 · StartYour first teardown this afternoon

      1. Scout. One request pulls the whole niche. Filter by days running, because nobody pays for months behind a losing ad.
      2. Break down. Every ad splits into hook, structure, proof, offer and funnel, with transcripts and the opening frames ready.
      3. Map. Hundreds of ads shrink into a map: which lever is overloaded, who owns what, where the space is.
      4. Produce. Angle, script, image, video and every format, in the same conversation. Replicate the structure, never the assets.
      5. Standardise. Brand voice, product data, the realism method and the report format, written as skills.
      6. Schedule. The morning report, the comments, the competitors. A tool becomes a system the moment you stop being the one who starts it.

      The advantage is no longer the bigger budget. It is how much faster you understand the market than your competitor does. Everyone can see the same ads, because they are public. The difference is that one person looks at a few a day, and the other reads a thousand at once and walks away with a map. And that advantage compounds with every niche you tear down.
